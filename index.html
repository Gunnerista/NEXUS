Scouting tools rate players. Agents get paid for knowing who will sign.

Wyscout can tell you a centre back wins 68 percent of his duels. It cannot tell you which club in the Ekstraklasa has a slot, a budget, a coach who wants a left footer, and a reason to move before the window closes. That second question is the job. NEXUS answers it in both directions.

Reverse Match

Player profile➔Ranked destinations

The selling side. A profile goes in, a tiered list of clubs comes out: contact now, follow up, monitor. Every club carries the evidence behind its rank, an outreach angle, and a flag on anything that could not be confirmed. Counterparties stay anonymous while a deal is live.

Built for the long tail. Our market is the Bulgarian top flight, Romanian second tier, the Gulf, K League, USL. Free agents on modest wages, in leagues where no clean database exists. A query engine needs tidy data. A reasoning agent reading local press in eight languages does not. That is why NEXUS is built as an agent, not a database.

Five gates. Every name passes all of them or it does not ship.

The order matters. The first gate kills the most fantasy, the last one kills the most deals. The questions are public. The thresholds are not.

  1. Tier realism

    Would this club really buy from this level, at this age, from this league?

    Criteria: sealed

  2. Player motive

    Would the player actually go? Minutes, money, family, the next contract.

    Criteria: sealed

  3. Buying pattern

    Does this club's own transfer history say it signs this profile, or only talks about it?

    Criteria: sealed

  4. Mobility this window

    Contract end, release terms, registration rules, foreign slots, window dates. Can he physically move now?

    Criteria: sealed

  5. Year one budget

    Fee plus wage plus everything else, in the club's currency, gross or net stated. Does it clear?

    Criteria: sealed

Then a sixth check that is not a gate: every surviving name is opened on Transfermarkt by the agent before output. What cannot be verified carries a Verify flag instead of a claim. Every finding carries a source and a date.

Every rule here was written by a miss.

The system does not get smarter by itself. It gets smarter because each failure is turned into a written rule and loaded into every session after it.

From Case #001, August 2026
A loud emergency is not a buyer.

Our top pick had the biggest injury headline in the league and had never heard of our player. The club that came to the table had quietly sold a roster slot and loaned out the same position eight days apart. We now rank relationship signals first, quiet preparation moves second, public emergencies last. A headline alone can never be the top pick.

The outreach doctrine
The first sentence is their problem, not our player.

A sporting director reads dozens of agent messages a day. "I have a player" is spam. "Your centre back deal collapsed last week" is information. The marketplaces sell that demand data by subscription. We build it from public signals ourselves, and if a need is not confirmed we ask instead of pitching.

From a live negotiation, August 2026
A deadline only works on someone who already wants the player.

We put a clock on a club before it had asked a single football question. The answer came the same day, and it was no. Before intent is confirmed the lever is scarcity, never time. Silence for four days drops the probability on the spot and the backup line starts.

The prediction ledger
Lock it, date it, never touch it.

Predictions are frozen the moment they are made. If a player turns out to have signed before the lock, the case is voided, not counted. Lessons from a miss change the next batch only. A self scoring system that edits itself is just marketing.

The query engine
"Experienced" is a question, not an input.

Briefs arrive at every level of detail. What the system can fill itself (rules, finances, squad, coach) it fills silently. What is genuinely ambiguous it asks, three to five questions at most. Nobody sends a sporting director a thirty item survey.

The operating model
One case, one session.

Each brief opens a clean workspace, so context from one deal never leaks into another. The agents carry forward only what was deliberately written down: rules, lessons, standing facts. Nothing else.

How one person runs a transfer desk.

The AI does the reading, the checking and the drafting. The human owns every relationship and every word that reaches a club. The line between them is written down, not improvised.

  1. A brief arrives by WhatsApp, email or a call, and goes into a fresh session in one line.
  2. A codified playbook loads. Each engine is a written skill, versioned and revised after every case.
  3. The agents research. Local press in eight languages, registration and quota rules, squad and contract data, buying history.
  4. Five gates and a Transfermarkt check. Unverified items are flagged, never smoothed over.
  5. A shortlist with outreach angles lands on a live board shared with my partner, so we work one picture, not two spreadsheets.
  6. The human decides and sends. Every message to a club is drafted by AI and approved by a person before it goes out.
  7. The outcome is logged. Contact, temperature, result. That private log is the demand layer no competitor can buy.

What the misses taught the next batch

  1. Medical history lowers confidence. One forward's move was decided by a failed medical, not by demand.
  2. Expensive veterans follow the wage, not the league. A market that can match the salary belongs in every top three.
  3. "Waiting for an offer from home" can be agent theatre. Press stories planted to raise a price are not destinations.
  4. When the obvious window shuts, players flow to big clubs that can sign all year. Two separate cases followed the same path.
  5. After October, predict one division lower. Free agents still unsigned in autumn land where clubs can still register them.
